Look at the websites of five firms in any sector and you will often find the same promises: quality, expertise, a personal service. Those things matter, but they are the price of entry. They do not help anyone decide between you and the next firm on the list.

A point of view does. It is a clear, honest statement of how you think your field should work, and it gives people something to agree with.

What a point of view is, and is not

A point of view is not a slogan or a mission statement. It is closer to an opinion you are prepared to defend. Good ones tend to share three qualities:

  1. They are specific to your field, not general statements about doing good work.
  2. Someone sensible could disagree with them.
  3. You can show them in how you work, not just in what you say.

Finding yours

Start with frustration. What do you see others in your sector doing that you think is wrong, or at least out of date? What do you find yourselves explaining to new clients again and again? The answers usually point to beliefs you already hold but have never written down.

Test it with your team

Share a draft with the people who deliver your work. If they recognise it and can give examples, you have something real. If they look puzzled, it is an aspiration, which is fine, but be careful about saying it in public until it is true.

Use it everywhere

A point of view earns its value through repetition. It should shape your website, your proposals, the articles you write and the way you answer the phone. Over time, people start to associate the idea with you.
