A purpose statement explains why an organisation exists beyond making money. Written well, it helps people make decisions, explains choices to clients and gives staff something to be proud of. Written badly, it becomes a line on the About page that nobody reads.

Common problems

The same issues come up again and again when purpose statements are drafted in a workshop. This table sets out the most common ones and what to do instead.

A simple structure

If you are stuck, try filling in this sentence: we exist to help [who] to [do or become what], so that [what changes]. It will not be your final wording, but it forces the important choices.

Then put it to work

  • Use it to say no to work that does not fit.
  • Mention it when you explain decisions to your team.
  • Revisit it once a year and ask whether it is still true.
