The quality of creative work depends heavily on the brief behind it. A vague brief leads to guesswork, extra rounds of changes and a result nobody is quite happy with. A clear one gives the people doing the work the best chance of getting it right first time.

What to include

  • The problem: what is not working, in your own words.
  • The audience: who you need to reach, and what they think now.
  • The outcome: what you want people to think, feel or do afterwards.
  • The limits: budget, deadlines and anything that cannot change.
  • The decision makers: who will sign the work off.

What to leave out

Resist the urge to describe the answer. A brief that says "we want a video" closes off options before anyone has looked at the problem. Describe what you need to achieve and let the agency suggest how.

Talk it through

Even the best written brief benefits from a conversation. Questions in the first meeting often uncover assumptions on both sides, and it is far cheaper to find them then than halfway through the project.
